# Exam Review Session

Today we went over the exam format and worked through a practice exam in class, so you get an idea of what to expect and how detailed your answers should be.

The exam is organized by method (GPR, ERT, Magnetometry, Airborne) plus a general concepts part. It mixes short conceptual questions with a few that ask you to read real data or sketch something. The idea is not to test memorization, but whether you understand what each method measures and how to apply that to a practical situation.

## Examples from the practice exam

**"How can you distinguish, in a radargram, between a reflection from a horizontal layer and a diffraction from a point object? Draw a small sketch."**
This is about reading a radargram, something we practiced with our own survey data.

**"How is the transition made from apparent resistivity values to a subsurface resistivity model? Explain in detail."**
Here we expect you to describe the inversion process, from the raw field measurements to the final resistivity model.

**"Investigating an archaeological site with walls, cavities, and metallic objects: what could ERT, GPR, and Magnetometry each contribute? Would you use one method or a combination?"**
This asks you to compare methods and justify a decision, like you would in a real field case.
