## Purpose

Throughout the course we worked with several geophysical prospecting methods. Each one measures a different physical property of the subsurface (electrical resistivity, dielectric permittivity, magnetic susceptibility, gamma radiation, etc.), so each is used for a different purpose. The goal of the report is to show these different applications: which method you used, what you used it for in the field, and what you found.

This is **not** meant to be a scientific paper. It's a technical report. That means we don't expect a long theoretical development or literature review. The theory should be explained briefly, just enough to understand what each method measures and why it was chosen for your objective. For example:

- ERT was used to characterize soil type at Kleinsee.
- GPR was used to detect buried utilities (pipes, cables, etc.).
- Magnetometry was used to detect magnetic anomalies related to ferrous metal objects.

(Replace these examples with the methods and objectives you actually worked with.)

## Expected Structure

1. **Introduction:** general context, what geophysical prospecting methods are, how they measure different physical parameters and are used for different purposes, and the objective of this report (to show the application of several methods in the field).
2. **Theoretical background (brief):** a short explanation of each method used, what it measures and its basic principle, without going into too much depth.
3. **Study area, equipment, and field procedure:** this is where we expect more detail. Where the survey was done, what equipment was used, acquisition parameters (electrode spacing, antenna frequency, line spacing, etc.), and how the fieldwork was carried out. Include photos and sketches of the area and the measurement setup.
4. **Data processing:** what processing steps were applied to the raw data (corrections, inversion, filtering, etc.) and with what software.
5. **Results:** present the results obtained for each method (maps, profiles, resistivity models, etc.).
6. **Discussion and conclusions:** interpret your results, what the anomalies mean, compare the methods (strengths and limitations), and close with your conclusions.
